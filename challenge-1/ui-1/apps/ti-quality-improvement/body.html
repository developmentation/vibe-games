Retrospectives

All TI delivery teams must run a retrospective at the end of every sprint or iteration. Retrospectives are the primary mechanism for surfacing quality improvement opportunities and must produce specific, assigned, and time-boxed action items tracked in the team's backlog.

Recommended format

  • What went well — identify practices the team wants to continue or amplify in the next sprint
  • What could be better — identify friction points, recurring blockers, or quality gaps
  • Action items — one to three specific, assigned, time-boxed improvements for the next sprint

Retrospective notes and action items must be stored in the project wiki or Confluence space and linked from the sprint summary. Action items not completed in the sprint they were assigned must be re-evaluated at the next retrospective — not silently dropped.

Quality metrics

All TI project teams are expected to track and review the following four key quality indicators each sprint. Results are reviewed monthly in the TI Quality dashboard.

Metric What it measures Target
Defect density Number of defects per 1,000 lines of new code delivered Less than 1.0
Test coverage Percentage of new lines covered by automated tests 80% or higher
Deployment frequency How often the team successfully deploys to production At least once per sprint
Mean time to recovery (MTTR) Average time to restore service after a production incident Under 4 hours

Teams significantly outside target ranges will be engaged by the TI Quality team to develop a time-bound improvement plan.

Technical debt management

Technical debt is a normal part of software development and must be actively managed. Each TI project must have a documented approach to identifying, prioritising, and reducing technical debt over time.

GoA approach

  • Maintain a dedicated technical debt backlog separate from the feature backlog
  • Reserve at least 20% of sprint capacity for debt reduction, refactoring, and dependency updates
  • Label debt items by category: architecture, code quality, dependency updates, test coverage, documentation
  • Use SonarCloud's maintainability rating to track code quality trends over time and surface high-value cleanup opportunities
  • Review the technical debt backlog in sprint planning — debt items must compete for priority alongside features, not be deferred indefinitely

Security-related debt is critical

Technical debt involving outdated dependencies with known CVEs, deprecated authentication mechanisms, or unencrypted data storage must be treated with the same urgency as a security finding. It cannot be deferred to a future sprint without an approved risk acceptance.

Post-incident reviews

All production incidents resulting in service degradation, data issues, or a security event must be followed by a blameless post-mortem within five business days of resolution.

Every post-mortem document must include:

  • A timeline of events from first symptom to full resolution
  • The root cause — not just the proximate trigger
  • What worked well in the detection and response
  • What could have been detected or resolved faster
  • Specific, assigned action items to prevent recurrence, tracked in the project backlog

Post-mortem summaries are shared with the broader TI community via the monthly quality update. Teams are encouraged to present learnings at the TI Community of Practice meetings.

Capability maturity self-assessment

Use the checklist below to assess your team's current quality maturity. Each practice represents a capability your team either has in place or is working toward. Gaps are improvement opportunities to raise in your next retrospective.

Practice Maturity level
All code changes go through a pull request with at least one reviewer Foundational
Unit tests are written alongside code and coverage is tracked in CI Foundational
CI pipeline runs on every push and blocks merges on failure Foundational
SAST and dependency scanning are automated in the CI pipeline Managed
End-to-end tests cover all primary user journeys Managed
Quality metrics are reviewed each sprint and trended over time Managed
Technical debt is actively tracked and at least 20% of sprint capacity is reserved for reduction Advanced
DAST scan passes before every production release Advanced
Post-incident reviews are completed within five business days and learnings are shared Advanced
Deployment frequency is at least once per sprint with no manual release steps Optimizing
MTTR is tracked and improving sprint-over-sprint through targeted reliability work Optimizing
